# STARTUP KNOWLEDGE — The Concepts That Matter

## CORE CONCEPTS

### MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
The smallest thing you can build that delivers value and tests your core hypothesis.
Not "minimum features" but "minimum learning." Build in days, not months. Ship, measure, learn.

### ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)
The specific person/company who has the problem you solve, feels the pain most acutely,
and is most likely to pay. NOT "everyone." A specific niche you can dominate first.

**Define your ICP:**
- Industry/role (e.g. "indie hackers building SaaS")
- Pain point (specific, not abstract)
- Willingness to pay (they already pay for something similar)
- Where they hang out (communities, channels)
- Why NOW (what changed that makes this possible)

### Product-Market Fit (PMF)
You have PMF when users would be "very disappointed" if your product disappeared (>40% on
the Sean Ellis test). Signs: organic growth, retention, users telling friends, people asking
to pay. If you have to push growth, you do not have PMF yet.

### Founder-Market Fit
Are YOU the right person to build THIS? Do you have domain expertise, network, passion, and
unfair advantage? Investors bet on founders who are uniquely qualified for their market.

## FUNDING STAGES

| Stage | Typical Amount | What You Need | What Investors Buy |
|-------|---------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Pre-seed | $100K-$1M | Idea + prototype + team | Team + vision |
| Seed | $1M-$3M | MVP + early traction (some revenue/users) | Market validation |
| Series A | $3M-$15M | PMF + repeatable growth engine | Scaling the engine |
| Series B+ | $15M-$100M+ | Proven unit economics + scaling | Market domination |

### Key Terms
- **SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity):** Investor gives money now, gets equity at next
  priced round. No valuation negotiation up front. Most common for pre-seed/seed.
- **Convertible Note:** Like a SAFE but it is debt that converts to equity. Has an interest rate
  and maturity date.
- **Bridge Round:** Between rounds, to extend runway. Usually from existing investors.
- **Cap Table:** Who owns what percentage of the company.
- **Liquidation Preference:** Who gets paid first (and how much) in an exit.
- **Pro-Rata:** Right to maintain your ownership percentage in future rounds.
- **Vesting:** Equity earned over time (typical: 4yr, 1yr cliff).

## UNIT ECONOMICS

| Metric | What It Means |
|--------|---------------|
| CAC | Customer Acquisition Cost. How much to get one paying user. |
| LTV | Lifetime Value. How much one user is worth over their entire time. |
| LTV:CAC | Must be >3:1 for a healthy business. <1:1 = burning money. |
| Payback Period | How many months until a user's revenue covers their CAC. Should be <12. |
| MRR | Monthly Recurring Revenue. The lifeblood of SaaS. |
| ARR | Annual Recurring Revenue (MRR x 12). |
| Churn Rate | % of users who leave per month. <5% monthly is good for early stage. |
| NRR | Net Revenue Retention. >100% means existing customers spend more over time. |
| Burn Rate | How much cash you spend per month. |
| Runway | Months until you run out of money (cash / monthly burn). |
| Gross Margin | Revenue minus cost of delivering the product. SaaS should be >70%. |

## MARKET SIZING

- **TAM (Total Addressable Market):** Everyone who could theoretically use your product.
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** The portion you can realistically reach.
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** What you can realistically capture in 3-5 years.
- If TAM < $1B, most VCs will not invest (too small). But a $100M SOM can be a great business.

## GROWTH MODELS

### Product-Led Growth (PLG)
The product drives acquisition, retention, and expansion. Users try free, convert themselves,
invite others. Examples: Slack, Figma, Notion, Calendly. Best for self-serve SaaS.

### Sales-Led Growth (SLG)
Humans drive acquisition. Sales team demos, negotiates, closes. Higher CAC, higher ACV.
Examples: Salesforce, HubSpot. Best for enterprise/B2B.

### Go-To-Market (GTM)
How you reach customers: SEO, content marketing, paid ads, viral loops, cold outreach,
partnerships, community, events. Pick 1-2 channels and dominate them before adding more.

## MOATS (Defensibility)

Why can't someone copy you and kill you?
- **Network Effects:** More users = more value (social networks, marketplaces)
- **Switching Costs:** Hard to leave (enterprise software with integrations)
- **Economies of Scale:** Cheaper at scale (Amazon, Cloudflare)
- **Brand:** People trust you (Apple, Stripe)
- **Technical:** Hard to replicate tech (Google search, AI models)
- **Regulatory:** Licenses/patents (fintech, biotech)
- **Data:** You have data others don't (recommendation engines)

## WEDGE

A wedge is the narrow entry point into a larger market. You solve ONE painful problem for
ONE specific user, then expand. Example: Superhuman started as "faster email for power users,"
now expanding into the broader email/productivity market.

## VIRALITY

- **Virality Coefficient (K):** K = invitations per user x conversion rate of invitations.
  K > 1 means viral growth (each user brings >1 new user).
- **Network Effects:** Value increases with each user.
- **Flywheel:** A self-reinforcing growth loop (more users -> more data -> better product ->
  more users).

## YC LOGIC (the essentials)

1. Build something people want (talk to users, not investors)
2. Ship fast, iterate faster (done > perfect)
3. Stay small, stay lean (do things that don't scale)
4. Focus on retention before acquisition (leaky bucket problem)
5. Revenue is the best funding (if you can, bootstrap)
6. Default alive > default dead (know your path to profitability)
7. Talk to 10 users a week, every week, forever
